P1Rope puzzle: 2 ropes each burn in exactly 60 minutes (non-uniform density). Measure exactly 45 minutes.LOW▾
Answer
Key constraint: ropes have non-uniform density — you cannot cut them and assume half length = half time. But you CAN light from both ends simultaneously.

Solution:
At t=0: Light Rope1 from BOTH ends. Light Rope2 from ONE end only.
Rope1 burns in 30 minutes (burning from both ends halves time regardless of density).
At t=30: Rope1 is gone. Immediately light Rope2's OTHER end.
Rope2 had 30 minutes of burn remaining. Burning from both ends: 15 minutes more.
At t=45: Rope2 is gone. Total = 45 minutes. ✓
💡 Insight
The key insight: burning from both ends doesn't change total material consumed — it halves the time remaining. This is a reasoning question about constraints (can't cut to measure time) and creative use of available actions (can light from multiple ends). When presented in interview, narrate your reasoning: 'I can't measure by length, but I can use the two-end burn trick to halve the remaining time at exactly the right moment.'
P2Bridge crossing: A=1min, B=2min, C=5min, D=8min. 2 people max, 1 torch, find minimum total time.LOW▾
Answer
Naive approach (wrong): A escorts everyone — 1+8+1+5+1+2 = 18 min.

Optimal solution (15 min):
Step 1: A+B cross together → 2 min. (Torch on far side, D and C waiting.)
Step 2: A returns with torch → 1 min. (Total: 3 min. C and D still waiting.)
Step 3: C+D cross together → 8 min. (Slowest pair cross together — key insight!)
Step 4: B returns with torch → 2 min. (Total: 13 min.)
Step 5: A+B cross together → 2 min. (Total: 15 min.) ✓

Why 15 not 17? Sending C and D together (Step 3) means D's 8 min only counts once, not twice. The greedy approach (A escorts each person) counts D's 8 min twice (going + returning A).
💡 Insight
The optimal insight: the bottleneck is the slowest pair (C and D). We must send them together to avoid paying their individual times twice. Then we need the fastest person (A or B) on the far side to return the torch. The classic greedy (fastest person escorts everyone) fails because it pays the slowest time for every escort return trip. This puzzle tests combinatorial optimisation thinking.
P3Water jugs: 3L and 5L containers. Measure exactly 4L of water.LOW▾
Answer
Solution (6 steps):
1. Fill 5L container completely.
2. Pour from 5L into 3L until 3L is full. 5L now has 2L. 3L is full.
3. Empty 3L completely.
4. Pour 2L from 5L into 3L. 3L has 2L. 5L is empty.
5. Fill 5L completely again.
6. Pour from 5L into 3L until 3L is full (needs 1L more). 5L now has 4L. ✓

State trace: (5L, 3L): (5,0)→(2,3)→(2,0)→(0,2)→(5,2)→(4,3). Done.
💡 Insight
General approach: BFS or mathematical — GCD(3,5)=1, so any value 1–8 is measurable with 3L and 5L containers. The algorithmic insight: pour from full to partially-full until one is empty or one is full, alternating sources. For interview narration: 'I know GCD(3,5)=1 so 4 is achievable. I'll track states: fill 5L, pour into 3L...' Shows systematic thinking.
P4Two buckets, one full of water, one empty. 3L and 5L mugs. Fill exactly 7L in the empty bucket.LOW▾
Answer
Minimum steps solution:
Strategy: 5+5-3 = 7. Fill the 5L mug twice, remove one 3L mug-full.

Step 1: Fill 5L mug from full bucket. Pour into empty bucket. (Bucket: 5L)
Step 2: Fill 5L mug again. Pour into empty bucket. (Bucket: 10L — too much)

Alternative: use subtraction approach:
Step 1: Fill 5L mug, pour into bucket (5L).
Step 2: Fill 3L mug, pour into bucket (8L).
Step 3: Fill 3L mug again, pour into bucket (11L — too much).

Correct minimal approach:
5 + 5 - 3 = 7: Fill 5L twice into bucket (10L), then remove 3L with 3L mug → 7L. ✓
💡 Insight
The key formula: 5×a - 3×b = 7. Smallest positive solution: a=2, b=1. So fill 5L mug twice and remove one 3L mug from the bucket. This is 3 total mug operations (2 fills of 5L + 1 removal of 3L) — the minimum. State equation approach generalises to any water puzzle.
P5Cake puzzle: cut a round cake into 8 equal pieces with exactly 3 cuts. All pieces must be equal.LOW▾
Answer
2D thinking fails: 3 straight cuts through the centre of a circle creates maximum 6 pieces (or 7 with non-centre cuts) — cannot reach 8 equal pieces with 3 vertical cuts.

3D solution:
Cut 1: Vertical cut through centre → 2 equal semicircles.
Cut 2: Perpendicular vertical cut through centre → 4 equal quarter-cylinders.
Cut 3: Horizontal cut through the MIDDLE of the cake height → 8 equal pieces. ✓

Each piece: same top surface area (quarter-circle), same height (half of cake). Equal in every dimension.
💡 Insight
This puzzle tests whether you think in 3D. Most people constrain themselves to 2D cuts. The horizontal cut perpendicular to the cake's axis (parallel to the table) is the non-obvious move. Narrate: 'I can't reach 8 with 3 vertical cuts through a circle. But there's no constraint saying cuts must be vertical — a horizontal cut through the middle doubles the pieces from 4 to 8.' Lateral thinking.
P6Chickens and rabbits: 72 heads, 200 feet. How many chickens and rabbits?LOW▾
Answer
Algebraic solution:
Let r = rabbits, c = chickens.
Heads: r + c = 72.
Feet: 4r + 2c = 200.

From equation 1: c = 72 - r.
Substitute: 4r + 2(72-r) = 200.
4r + 144 - 2r = 200.
2r = 56. r = 28.
c = 72 - 28 = 44.

Verify: 28 × 4 + 44 × 2 = 112 + 88 = 200 ✓. 28 + 44 = 72 ✓.

Shortcut (no algebra): If all chickens: 72×2=144 feet. Have 200-144=56 extra feet. Each rabbit contributes 4-2=2 extra feet over a chicken. 56/2=28 rabbits. 72-28=44 chickens.
💡 Insight
The shortcut method is faster in an interview: assume all are the same type (chickens), calculate feet, find the deficit or surplus, and calculate how many swaps (chicken→rabbit) fix the discrepancy. This approach generalises: any 'two types with different counts per individual' problem. Also appeared as: '100 balls, some worth Rs.5, others Rs.10, total Rs.700 — how many of each?'
P73 switches outside a room, 1 bulb inside. Enter room only once. Which switch controls the bulb?LOW▾
Answer
Solution using heat as extra information channel:
Step 1: Turn Switch 1 ON and wait 10 minutes.
Step 2: Turn Switch 1 OFF.
Step 3: Turn Switch 2 ON.
Step 4: Enter the room.

Now observe:
Bulb ON → Switch 2 controls it (currently on).
Bulb OFF and WARM to touch → Switch 1 controls it (was on for 10 min, recently turned off).
Bulb OFF and COLD → Switch 3 controls it (never turned on).
💡 Insight
This puzzle demonstrates multi-channel information gathering. Binary state (ON/OFF) gives 2 states for 2 switches — not enough for 3. The bulb's temperature is a third information channel (warm = recently on, cold = never on). In verification, this parallel: a DUT output signal alone may not distinguish all states — you need additional observable signals (temperature analogue = timing, order of events, side-channel outputs) to fully verify behaviour.
P8Person buys Rs.300 item with fake Rs.1000 note. Shopkeeper gives Rs.700 change + item. Fake note returned. What is shopkeeper's total loss?LOW▾
Answer
Trace all value flows:
Person gives shopkeeper: Rs.0 real value (fake note = worthless).
Shopkeeper gives person: Rs.300 item + Rs.700 change = Rs.1000 real value out.
Fake note returned: person gives back Rs.0 real value.

Total loss = Rs.1000.

Alternative view: shopkeeper's net position before = 0. After = -Rs.1000 (lost Rs.700 cash from drawer + Rs.300 merchandise). Fake note in hand = Rs.0. Net: -Rs.1000.
💡 Insight
The trap: some people think about the exchange with the change-giving shop (some versions have a shopkeeper getting change elsewhere). In the simple version, the answer is Rs.1000 total. The key insight: trace real value flows, not paper money flows. The fake note is Rs.0 value. Everything the shopkeeper gave out was real value. Nothing real came in.
