Common Mistakes to Avoid

Vague project explanation
'I verified AXI' is not an answer. 'I verified multi-master arbitration in the NoC fabric — specifically simultaneous 3-master collision to same slave, priority preemption mid-grant, and address decode boundary conditions' is an answer. Specificity = credibility.
Cannot write code on paper
10+ interviews in this database had live coding on notepad. AMD, Google, Intel, Qualcomm ask you to write driver/constraint/assertion on paper with no IDE. Practice daily for 1 week before any interview. If you can only code in an IDE, you will fail.
Bluffing on GLS or formal
Discovered in 2 follow-up questions. Say instead: 'I haven't run GLS personally but I understand it simulates the post-synthesis netlist with SDF timing to catch setup/hold violations and X-propagation that RTL simulation misses.' Honest awareness beats discovered bluff every time.
Not knowing your resume
Every word on your resume is a commitment. If it says NoC — prepare round-robin arbitration at signal level. If it says DMA — prepare descriptor pipeline at register level. Interviewers read resumes and target exactly what's written. Interview rule: never put something on your resume you can't explain at signal level for 10 minutes.
Generic OOP examples
'Animals make different sounds' fails at AMD and Meta. Give the TB example: 'Error injection driver that extends base driver, overrides drive_txn() to corrupt data with 10% probability, registered via factory in error test only — zero changes to env or agent.' Shows you understand why virtual functions and factory exist together.
No corner cases prepared
Always 3+ corner cases per project minimum. 'Read and write' are NOT corner cases — those are basic functionality. Corner cases: simultaneous events at same clock edge, exact boundary conditions, reset mid-transaction, error recovery paths. This is what separates 5-year from 3-year engineers in interviews.
Freezing on puzzles
When a puzzle arrives: say 'Let me think through this' and narrate your reasoning aloud. Interviewers give partial credit for correct reasoning even if the answer is slightly off. Silence followed by wrong answer = 0 marks. Narrated reasoning that reaches wrong answer = partial credit. Reasoning is the test, not the answer.
Not asking about the role
At the end of every interview: ask 'What IP or SoC will I be working on?' and 'What is the team's primary verification methodology — UVM constrained random, formal, or hybrid?' This shows genuine engineering interest and opens a conversation where you can position your experience against their actual need. It also signals you care about the work, not just the offer.

Final Reminders Before Every Interview

  • Look up the company's recent product announcements the day before — mention it naturally in the conversation
  • Know your project's block-level signal names cold — not just 'input signals' but the actual RTL signal names
  • Prepare one specific bug you found with full detail: symptom → debug → root cause → fix → confirmation
  • Know the answer to 'why did you join Auxera?' and 'what kind of role are you looking for?' — these are often the opening questions
  • Have your resume in front of you during the call — interviewers read from it and you should be able to quickly locate what they're referencing
  • End every interview by thanking the interviewer and asking for a timeline on next steps
